# OJT Final Reflections
**Internship Journey: Development of the School Clinic Management System**

---

## I. Potential of the Company as a Training Ground

The institution has demonstrated outstanding potential as a training ground for Information Technology students. Throughout my thirteen weeks of internship, I was exposed to a wide range of real-world challenges that classroom learning alone cannot fully prepare a student for. Rather than assigning interns to simple observational roles, the institution entrusted me with the full development of the School Clinic Management System, covering every phase from planning and design to deployment and user training.

The environment is multidisciplinary, offering exposure to both software development and physical network infrastructure such as router configuration and LAN cabling. The presence of real end users, including medical staff and school administrators, created an authentic professional atmosphere where I could practice communication, feedback handling, and stakeholder management. The administration's openness to innovative ideas further encouraged me to take initiative and propose creative solutions. Overall, this institution is a highly effective and well-rounded training ground for aspiring IT professionals.

---

## II. Suggestions for the Improvement of the Training Program

Based on my internship experience, I would like to offer the following suggestions to further improve the training program. First, a structured onboarding orientation should be provided at the start of every internship to help incoming trainees understand the institution's infrastructure, ongoing projects, and expectations. This would significantly reduce adjustment time and allow interns to contribute more effectively from the very beginning.

Second, the program should incorporate version control tools such as Git into the intern's workflow to teach industry-standard collaborative development practices. Third, a formal weekly progress review with the OJT supervisor should be established to ensure trainees are on the right track and to provide timely guidance. Finally, dedicated time for self-directed learning should be built into the program schedule, encouraging interns to research new technologies and continuously develop their skills beyond their assigned tasks.

---

## III. Advice to Future On-the-Job Trainees

To future OJT trainees, my most important advice is to come prepared but remain humble. Review your core IT subjects before your internship begins, but always be ready to learn things you have never encountered in the classroom. Take initiative by volunteering for tasks and proposing solutions rather than waiting to be told what to do at every step.

Do not be afraid to ask questions. Asking thoughtful questions shows curiosity and professionalism, not weakness. Treat every task as a learning opportunity, even the repetitive ones like data entry, because every task carries a hidden lesson about real-world systems. Always document your daily work and learnings in a personal journal, as this will make writing your reports easier and serve as a future reference. Most importantly, give your best in every output, because the systems you build will be used by real people who depend on their reliability and quality.
